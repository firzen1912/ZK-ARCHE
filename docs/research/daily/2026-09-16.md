## 2. Executive synthesis

1. **Delegation validity should be an ordered, attenuating, key-continuous chain, not a bag of independently valid grants.** Current July/August 2026 IETF delegation drafts converge on per-hop signature/key continuity, authority containment, audience/time downscoping, bounded depth, and holder proof-of-possession. This strongly corroborates ZK-ARCHE's bounded P2P direction without making OAuth infrastructure a dependency.
2. **Revoking an intermediate delegation should invalidate its descendant subtree, while retaining the historical chain for audit does not preserve authorization.** The June 2026 OAuth delegation-chain draft states this explicitly; current ZK-ARCHE lineage replacement already treats predecessor-bound dependent state as authority that must be invalidated before successor activation.
3. **Offline delegation creates an unavoidable revocation-freshness tradeoff.** Recent attenuating-token work makes the trade explicit: offline verification reduces online dependency but weakens immediate per-token revocation unless short lifetimes, cached status, generations, or another freshness mechanism bounds stale authority. ZK-ARCHE should keep its infrastructure-independent baseline, but must measure/profile the maximum stale-delegation window rather than implying instantaneous global revocation.
4. No backlog status transition is justified. R-008/R-012 remain `reproduce`: the repository still needs deterministic Rust/C descendant-revocation vectors, bounded chain-size/verification-cost measurements, and a stateful formal property tying ancestor invalidation to descendant DATA denial.

## 5. Findings

### D20260916-F01 — Delegation chain validity requires attenuation, adjacent key continuity, and leaf holder binding

- **Backlog:** R-008; **novelty:** corroborates/refines.
- **Primary sources:** `draft-li-oauth-delegated-authorization-03` (July 2026); `draft-asor-wimse-agent-delegation-chain-00` (August 2026); RFC 9449 (DPoP).
- **Verified source-supported claim:** the current delegated-authorization draft validates ordered token chains, restrictions at every hop, adjacent key continuity, depth, permission/audience/time downscoping, and proof of possession by the leaf holder. The August WIMSE draft independently requires parent linkage, non-increasing authority/time, bounded depth, and holder binding.
- **Repository fact:** ZK-ARCHE's current work is already moving toward bounded P2P delegation and explicit enrollment/delegation trust boundaries, while its Common Contract requires local authentication/authorization without an always-online CA/cloud dependency.
- **Engineering idea:** define delegated authority as `root authority ∩ hop_1 constraints ∩ ... ∩ leaf constraints`, with each hop cryptographically bound to the next holder and with an explicit maximum depth. Unknown security constraints must fail closed rather than disappear during attenuation.
- **Evidence maturity:** current IETF work-in-progress plus mature PoP RFC; ZK-ARCHE mapping is software/conformance-target research.
- **Limitations:** these drafts target OAuth/agent ecosystems and are not ZK-ARCHE dependencies; their wire formats and online AS assumptions should not be imported into the constrained baseline.
- **Impact:** wire/CPU/RAM scale approximately with chain depth unless ZK-ARCHE uses a compact derived representation; flash/persistent state depends on revocation strategy. Trust expands with each delegator, so a depth ceiling is both a resource and trust bound. Privacy can worsen if full lineage is disclosed to every peer.
- **Rust/C/vector impact:** add exact parent-child key-continuity, scope/audience/time attenuation, depth overflow, unknown constraint, reordered hop, and leaf-PoP negatives with cross-language parity.
- **Required next evidence:** canonical ZK-ARCHE delegation representation; bounded chain-size and verification-cost measurements; Rust/C differential corpus.
- **Disposition:** reproduce.

### D20260916-F02 — Intermediate revocation invalidates descendants; audit retention is not authority retention

- **Backlog:** R-012; **novelty:** refines.
- **Primary sources:** `draft-liu-oauth-chain-delegation-00` (June 2026); `draft-li-oauth-delegated-authorization-03` (July 2026); RFC 9594 (ACE group key provisioning).
- **Verified source-supported claim:** the June delegation-chain draft states that revoking an intermediate hop invalidates tokens derived downstream from that hop. The July delegated-authorization draft separates cryptographic chain provenance from current usability. RFC 9594 requires rekeying when removal is needed for forward security, showing that authorization removal and key state must converge rather than merely marking a record revoked.
- **Repository fact:** current lineage replacement already treats predecessor-bound resumption and other dependent authority as invalidation obligations and forbids successor activation when required invalidation is missing.
- **Engineering idea:** model delegation revocation as subtree authority invalidation keyed by lineage/generation, while retaining historical provenance as non-authoritative audit evidence. Ancestor replacement/revocation must invalidate descendant cached authorization, resumption, and DATA-release authority before those descendants can be treated as current.
- **Evidence maturity:** standards/draft evidence; ZK-ARCHE inference is a reproduction/formal target.
- **Limitations:** centralized OAuth introspection is incompatible with ZK-ARCHE's infrastructure-independent mandatory baseline; subtree invalidation must have an offline-capable representation.
- **Impact:** bounded generation/revocation metadata adds persistent state; eager subtree enumeration can be expensive, so generation-root invalidation or compact ancestry commitments deserve comparison. Privacy argues against exposing full delegation lineage when a compact proof suffices.
- **Rust/C/vector impact:** ancestor revoke → descendant deny; sibling remains valid where policy permits; lineage replacement → old descendants deny; retained audit record → still deny; restart/rollback stale-generation negatives.
- **Required next evidence:** exact descendant invalidation algorithm, bounded persistent metadata model, Rust/C lifecycle corpus, Tamarin/ProVerif state property.
- **Disposition:** reproduce.

### D20260916-F03 — Offline delegation needs an explicit bounded stale-revocation contract

- **Backlog:** R-012 / R-008; **novelty:** refines.
- **Primary sources:** `draft-niyikiza-oauth-attenuating-agent-tokens-01` (June 2026); RFC 9770 (ACE revoked-token notification); RFC 9594.
- **Verified source-supported claim:** current attenuating-token work explicitly trades per-token immediate revocation for offline verifiability and uses bounded lifetime as a recovery mechanism. RFC 9770 supplies constrained revocation notifications/TRL synchronization as an alternative to per-request introspection. RFC 9594 treats membership removal and rekey as lifecycle operations whose convergence can temporarily be misaligned.
- **Repository fact:** R-012 already requires a maximum stale-authorization window per profile and offline/degraded reconnect behavior; this remains unresolved.
- **Engineering idea:** each profile should declare a measurable revocation-freshness contract, e.g. maximum accepted revocation-generation age, maximum delegation lifetime/generation distance, and the condition that forces fresh synchronization/full AUTH before protected DATA. No profile should imply instantaneous revocation when disconnected.
- **Evidence maturity:** standards plus work-in-progress; ZK-ARCHE mapping is benchmark/reproduction target.
- **Limitations:** wall-clock expiry may be unsuitable on some MCUs; rollback-safe monotonic generations can substitute only when their persistence and recovery are actually qualified.
- **Impact:** shorter validity reduces stale-authority risk but increases reauthorization traffic/energy; cached revocation generations consume bounded persistent state; privacy depends on synchronization identifiers and update cadence.
- **Rust/C/vector impact:** stale-but-not-expired delegation, missed revocation generation, reconnect catch-up, rollback to older revocation state, forced full-AUTH threshold, and DATA denial during unresolved synchronization.
- **Required next evidence:** profile-specific stale-window budget, disconnected/reconnect benchmark, persistent-generation rollback evidence on target hardware.
- **Disposition:** benchmark/reproduce.

## 6. Cross-source synthesis

The sources converge on a useful ZK-ARCHE boundary: **delegation is current authority only when chain provenance, attenuation, holder binding, and revocation freshness all remain valid.** Cryptographically valid historical lineage is valuable for audit but is not sufficient for current authorization. This aligns with the repository's broader doctrine that AUTH, cached authorization, resumption, lineage, and DATA authority are separate domains that must all be current.

For the Common Contract, the transferable ideas are semantic rather than OAuth-specific: bounded depth, monotonic attenuation, adjacent holder/key continuity, explicit descendant invalidation, and a measurable offline stale-authority bound. Online introspection, centralized authorization servers, or agent-specific token formats should remain optional comparators rather than mandatory dependencies.
