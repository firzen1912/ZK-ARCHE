## 2. Executive synthesis

1. The October 5 ANIMA voucher-artifact Internet-Draft, revision 37, explains how the same manufacturing serial number can refer to different devices when issuer domains differ. Its specific `idevid-issuer` rules provide a **comparison and negative-test design**, not a required ZK-ARCHE field.
2. ZK-ARCHE already requires one authoritative local identity attribution and cross-selector agreement. The decision gap is to make issuer/authority namespace collisions and the exact canonical security-object equivalence reproducible in Rust/C across reference, alias and cache paths.
3. RFC 8995 and constrained BRSKI distinguish an initial provisional connection from subsequently authorized trust. ZK-ARCHE's NO-LEARNING normal AUTH and explicit ENROLL lifecycle are consistent in direction, but neither local decision vectors nor logical storage-order tests establish durable alias+credential+replay atomicity on real storage.
4. The current voucher draft treats nonce-based freshness and expiration-based freshness as alternative constructions; no-clock peers cannot validate wall-clock expiry merely from a received timestamp. ZK-ARCHE should preserve its independent enrollment replay/authorization-generation authority and test repeated boots and clone/rollback.
5. R-013 remains **P0 / reproduce**. No specification, protocol, trust root, mandatory suite, wire format, roadmap, physical qualification, or security maturity is promoted.

### D20261018-F01 — Issuer-scoped identity and canonical alias equivalence

- **Novelty / owner:** refines R-013; `spec/implementation-requirements.md` §4.1; TD-004.
- **Primary source / type / date:** IETF ANIMA Working Group Internet-Draft `draft-ietf-anima-rfc8366bis-37`, *A Voucher Artifact for Onboarding Protocols*, **2026-10-05** (work in progress), §§6.1–6.3.
- **Link:** https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-anima-rfc8366bis-37
- **Source-supported claim:** the draft requires `idevid-issuer` in its registrar voucher request and specifies a full DER AuthorityKeyIdentifier representation. It explains that device serial numbers may collide across manufacturing lines/issuers or a shared manufacturer authorizing service. This is a specific BRSKI/voucher contract, not a universal identity grammar.
- **Repository fact / bottleneck:** ZK-ARCHE's authoritative local attribution model already rejects ambiguous or conflicting mappings; currently retained canonical vectors do not demonstrate cross-issuer identical-ID and inconsistent canonical alias/reference/cache representations as a complete end-to-end enrolled trust transition.
- **Distinct engineering idea / ZK-ARCHE inference:** construct a **synthetic issuer-scoped identity collision matrix**. For equal local subject labels/opaque identifiers under different enrollment-authority domains, require explicit authenticated authority namespace and security-object identity before merging references. Test byte-distinct but semantically claimed identical aliases, forged namespace, swapped public key/commitment, stale cache, cross-session reuse and references resolving to multiple subjects; preserve separately authorized same-key reuse only with exactly one selected attribution tuple.
- **Evidence maturity:** concept informed by an official standards-track draft and existing software contracts; no new ZK-ARCHE negative corpus executed; issuer-collision applicability depends on actual provisioning models.
- **Limits:** `idevid-issuer` DER/serial rules belong to BRSKI; copying them to certificate-free ZK-ARCHE would impose unjustified X.509 parsing and semantic commitments. This does not demonstrate a present exploit.
- **Resource / dependency / trust / privacy impact:** no mandatory wire change; a local namespace discriminator, canonical validation and cross-path comparison may add bounded CPU/RAM/flash depending on identity representation; an externally visible stable issuer+identifier would **increase correlation risk** (R-015); no new CA/MASA trust dependency.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile impact:** preserve `iot-core` profile and existing positive fixtures; add shared adversarial collision/alias/parity decisions with exact byte encodings and hash/provenance of the selected local attribution, without changing protocol identity rules automatically.
- **Required evidence:** an exact-head Rust/C differential corpus for two independent issuers with equal identifiers, alias/reference/cache crossover, contradictory key/role/audience tuples, consistent intentional key reuse, and post-restart mutation visibility.
- **Disposition / weekly:** reproduce / yes.

### D20261018-F02 — Provisional connection is not a committed trust anchor

- **Novelty / owner:** refines R-013; `spec/auth-trust-mutation-boundary.md`, `spec/enroll-lifecycle-state-machine.md`.
- **Primary sources / dates / types:** RFC 8995, *BRSKI*, **May 2021**, §5.6; IETF ANIMA `draft-ietf-anima-constrained-voucher-31`, *cBRSKI*, **2026-06-08**, §§14.3–14.4 (work in progress).
- **Links:** https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8995.html ; https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-anima-constrained-voucher-31
- **Source-supported claim:** in BRSKI the initial registrar TLS connection is provisional; the pledge validates an authorized voucher and the pinned domain credential before treating the registrar as authenticated. cBRSKI adapts this to constrained CoAP/(D)TLS and compact CBOR vouchers. These standards use manufacturer-related authority, unlike ZK-ARCHE's infrastructure-independent local-trust floor.
- **Repository fact / bottleneck:** ZK-ARCHE forbids normal-AUTH learning, and explicit ENROLL distinguishes authorized issuance from durable commit. Logical Rust/C lineage replacement storage-order tests are not physical trust-store journaling tests or proof of coherent alias and replay-operation-ID publication.
- **Distinct engineering idea / ZK-ARCHE inference:** enforce a **provisional-to-authoritative visibility barrier**: an attempt-scoped candidate binding remains unusable to ordinary AUTH, concurrent AUTH, P2P, resumption or cached lookups until the existing locally authorized ENROLL mutation owner confirms authority and the durable transaction commits the credential, aliases, consumed operation ID, and lineage/generation together. Failed, interrupted or mismatched attempts neither leak provisional trust nor overwrite an existing authoritative record.
- **Evidence maturity:** software/spec-level comparison, no end-to-end ZK-ARCHE transaction/power-loss reproduction; no inference of stronger real-hardware durability.
- **Limitations:** BRSKI's vendor/MASA/PKI and provisional TLS handshake are *not* requirements to add to ZK-ARCHE. This finding does not show an actual violation of current runtime code.
- **Resource / dependency / trust / privacy impact:** no mandatory wire changes; attempt-scoped RAM and durable state/journal metadata may increase RAM, flash writes, CPU, energy and recovery latency; isolation protects local trust non-transitivity; error/commit visibility should be measured for R-015.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile impact:** add shared interleaved two-attempt and AUTH-vs-ENROLL decisions: aborted staging, wrong issuer, duplicated operation ID, torn alias/credential write, stale generation at commit, power-cut at every persistence step, and safe identical-commit replay. Keep existing profile semantics unchanged.
- **Required evidence:** exact-head concurrent Rust/C software adapter fault corpus plus physical power-cut/restart evidence for any target-specific claim.
- **Disposition / weekly:** reproduce / yes.

### D20261018-F03 — Freshness without an authenticated clock

- **Novelty / owner:** refines R-008; R-013 lifecycle consistency cross-link.
- **Primary source / type / date:** `draft-ietf-anima-rfc8366bis-37`, IETF ANIMA Internet-Draft, **2026-10-05**, voucher `nonce`/`expires-on` schema and §11.1; https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-anima-rfc8366bis-37
- **Source-supported claim:** the voucher draft prohibits a MASA from including both `nonce` and `expires-on` in the same voucher; clockless pledges cannot safely validate a wall-clock expiration and may use a fresh nonce-based voucher method. This is **not** a general prohibition on mixing freshness evidence in all protocols.
- **Repository fact / bottleneck:** ZK-ARCHE already requires unused ENROLL operation ID and authenticated commissioner generation provenance, bound to durable commit. These cannot be inferred from a successful AUTH or a sender-provided timestamp.
- **Distinct engineering idea / inference:** retain two *profile-specific* freshness predicates: operation-ID-bound freshness for no-clock devices versus qualified authenticated time when available. Test clone/boot nonce reuse separately from durable consumed-ID state, commissioner generation and revocation view, and fail closed on uncertainty. Do not collapse nonce, time, replay cache and authorization generation into one Boolean.
- **Evidence maturity:** concept/standards comparison; no ZK-ARCHE target or restart test executed. No guarantee that entropy or durable monotonic storage exists on the intended MCU.
- **Wire / resource / trust / privacy:** no baseline field or suite change; nonce tracking and rollback-resistant operation-ID retention can cost RAM/flash/CPU/energy; observable retries and identifiers need R-015 review; local trust authority is unchanged.
- **Rust/C / vectors / profile:** add profile-matched, shared no-clock reboot/duplicate and trusted-time negative cases without adopting BRSKI's 8–32-byte voucher nonce range as a ZK-ARCHE protocol requirement.
- **Required evidence:** exact-head Rust/C lifetime, restart, clone and multi-attempt corpus; physical RNG/storage validation is R-005/TD-002.
- **Disposition / weekly:** reproduce / no (cross-link to existing R-008 evidence).

## 6. Cross-source synthesis

The common lesson is **authority provenance precedes persistent identity reuse**. An issuer namespace distinguishes otherwise colliding device labels; a provisional connection or valid possession proof does not make a credential authoritative; freshness evidence must be checked under the *selected* trust and clock model. ZK-ARCHE already has appropriate draft boundaries but still needs executable cross-path, concurrency and persistence proofs. Do not promote BRSKI manufacturer/PKI assumptions into the mandatory certificate-free Common Contract.
