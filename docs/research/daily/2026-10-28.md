## 2. Executive synthesis

1. **New primary comparator (21 October 2026):** the W3C Bitstring Status List v1.1 Working Draft adds a `TerseBitstringStatusListEntry`, requires a `2^26` entry list length for its terse conversion, and explicitly discusses herd-population privacy, cross-verifier status-index correlation, local caching and distinguishable decoys. It is work in progress, not an adopted ZK-ARCHE protocol.
2. **Size is a security-boundary choice, not just a compression statistic:** a normal one-bit 131,072-entry list expands to at least 16 KiB; a one-bit `2^26` list expands to 8 MiB. Neither a tiny gzip transfer nor a terse reference proves bounded verifier RAM, flash, decompression work or link privacy on an STM32/ESP32-S3.
3. **Refresh is not authority:** v1.1 separates `ttl` refresh guidance from the validity period; the June 2026 OAuth Token Status List draft separately distinguishes signed status, expiry, refresh bounds and issuer tracking. Neither a cache hit nor HTTP success establishes ZK-ARCHE authority-scope, monotonic `view_epoch`, no-clock freshness or absence of rollback.
4. Retain R-012 as `reproduce` with a revised **privacy/resource/currentness three-axis comparator** and distinct Rust/C negative/fault vectors; R-015 remains unchanged. No rollout, new wire format, altered revocation action semantics or readiness advancement.

## 5. Findings

### D20261028-F01 — Nominal list size is not effective privacy population

- **Novelty / owners:** refines R-012's batched-status comparator and cross-links R-015; no new research ID.
- **Primary source, type, date, link:** W3C Verifiable Credentials WG, *Bitstring Status List v1.1*, W3C Working Draft, **2026-10-21**: https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-vc-bitstring-status-list-1.1-20261021/ ; §§2.3, 6.1–6.5. Earlier v1.0 was W3C Recommendation, 2025-05-15: https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-bitstring-status-list/ .
- **Verified source-supported claim:** v1.1 adds terse entry/index conversion with `listLength = 2^26`; its privacy guidance warns that a large nominal bitstring provides inadequate herd privacy with few real issued credentials, that `statusListIndex`/`statusListCredential` can correlate holders across verifiers, and that local caching or distributed hosting can reduce direct issuer observation. Distinguishable decoys do not enlarge the effective anonymity set. The draft remains work in progress.
- **Repository fact / bottleneck:** ZK-ARCHE `revocation-view-representation.md` uses authority-scoped opaque holder/lineage references and a bounded REVOKE-only set; `privacy-considerations.md` does not claim session-level unlinkability or private status lookup. The present source of authority is local configured trust, not a status-provider URL.
- **Distinct engineering idea:** add a paired privacy/resource experiment in which the *same* authenticated monotonic revocation semantics are expressed by (a) bounded sparse REVOKE entries, (b) batched indexed bitsets, and (c) a cache of opaque authority-scoped revocation views. Vary **real population** independently of allocated indexes, index rotation, issuer/scope partitions, changes per epoch, decoy distinguishability, distribution fetch cadence and observing-party vantage. Measure actual issuer-to-device and peer-to-peer linkability, not just padding size.
- **Evidence maturity:** source is a public W3C Working Draft; ZK-ARCHE application is **concept**, not software, constrained-hardware or deployed evidence.
- **Limitations / uncertainty:** W3C privacy guidance is substantially nonnormative and assumes a VC issuer/holder/verifier ecosystem; the ZK-ARCHE trust and enrollment model differs. A sufficiently large list does not prove unlinkability and a stable index may be useful for fast checks yet privacy-sensitive. No privacy experiment was executed.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** if indexed status information is ever considered, status references must be explicitly scoped to accepted authority, deployment, profile and generation and must not become protocol identity or a second trust root. Passive/active observers could correlate repeated references even when AUTH's main pseudonym rotates; data and lookup traces are needed to determine actual advantage.
- **Wire / CPU / RAM / flash / dependency / trust / privacy impact:** no existing wire change. Indexed status might reduce typical batch transfer while requiring a status-entry/index reference, cache and decompressor; stable references may harm privacy. Any W3C-specific VC/JSON-LD/DID/HTTP dependency is **not** acceptable as a mandatory ZK-ARCHE baseline absent human review and MCU measurements. Operational savings and leakage remain unmeasured.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile impact:** plan a shared negative corpus for wrong-issuer indexes, index reuse/rotation, cross-deployment list substitution, wrong generation, status-list scope mismatch, stale cached views, decoy distinguishability and linkability traces; no changes to existing v1 REVOKE semantics or vectors today.
- **Required next evidence / disposition / weekly:** attack manifest with real-credential population, list allocation, sampling/observability and confidence intervals; paired sparse/indexed trace results, Rust/C interpretation parity and no-cloud/offline safety. **reproduce; yes**.

### D20261028-F02 — Decompressed size and validity/currentness are independent

- **Novelty / owners:** refines R-012 with a concrete 2026 resource/freshness comparator; cross-link R-005 for MCU qualification.
- **Primary sources, types, dates, links:** W3C, *Bitstring Status List v1.1*, Working Draft **2026-10-21**, §§2.2–2.3, 3.3–3.4, 7: https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-vc-bitstring-status-list-1.1-20261021/ . IETF OAuth WG, Looker/Bastian/Bormann, *Token Status List (TSL)*, `draft-ietf-oauth-status-list-21`, **2026-06-21**, active Internet-Draft: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list-21 , §§4.1, 11.3–11.5, 12.
- **Verified source-supported claim:** the W3C one-bit minimum is 131,072 entries = **16,384 uncompressed bytes**; its terse conversion fixes a **2^26-entry** list, whose minimum one-bit uncompressed representation is **8,388,608 bytes (8 MiB)**. Compressed transfer size is not expanded verifier memory usage; v1.1's `ttl` advises refresh and does **not** override list validity. OAuth TSL uses protected JWT/CWT status lists with 1/2/4/8 bits per entry, compressed content and issuer/signature checks; it cautions that too-small `ttl`/`exp` can create fetch storms. These are comparator-specific formats, not ZK-ARCHE requirements.
- **Repository fact / bottleneck:** `spec/revocation-update-ingestion.md` already requires pre-authoritative limits on entry count, payload and reconciliation scratch; rejects oversize updates without partial incorporation; demands atomic visibility of an epoch with its complete revocation set. `spec/revocation-convergence-and-stale-authorization.md` requires deterministic profile freshness and fails closed when stale. `spec/revocation-view-representation.md` has **REVOKE-only, no implicit unrevocation** v1 logic. It does not adopt gzip/bitsets, COSE/JWT status or a wire list.
- **Strongest distinct engineering idea:** qualify candidate distribution shapes using a bounded **`compressed_input_bytes`, `authenticated_object_bytes`, `max_decompressed_bytes`, `peak_scratch_bytes`, `CPU_cycles`, `flash_writes`, `cache_bytes`** evidence tuple, together with independent **authenticity/authority, monotonic epoch/reconciliation and profile-local freshness** checks. Compare encrypted/signed compressed remote cache vs preverified bounded local sparse views. Never make `ttl`, new HTTP response, refreshed TLS or cache revalidation equivalent to a new trusted revocation epoch.
- **Evidence maturity:** **concept** for ZK-ARCHE; external specs available, no Rust/C decompression/MCU run. The byte counts above are arithmetic from specified logical lengths, not ZK-ARCHE measurements.
- **Limitations:** streaming/partial decoding can sometimes reduce scratch, but must be shown to preserve authentication before authoritative incorporation, full-list completeness, exact base-epoch reconciliation and bounded CPU; integer overflow and compression bombs need separate testing. Clockless targets cannot automatically apply wall-clock `exp` as a safe currentness proof. W3C permits additional status purposes that cannot be mapped silently into v1's monotonic REVOKE-only semantics.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** an attractive compressed batch may exceed iot-core's reserved memory/persistence envelope after expansion; for a small offline peer, a bounded sparse authenticated view or higher-capability distribution-side compression may be preferable without outsourcing the final local authorization decision. The trust and freshness checks must be independent of the chosen representation, and invalidating an old view must propagate to session/resumption/delegation/DATA consumers.
- **Wire / CPU / RAM / flash / dependency / trust / privacy impact:** no immediate wire change; experimental options may add transport-specific wire framing, CPU for decompression/signature verification, peak RAM at least equal to needed materialized bytes unless streamed safely, flash wear, extra metadata and dependency surface. The 16 KiB/8 MiB logical payloads are not suitable as mandatory worst-case in-core buffers for tiny peers without measured feasibility. Fetch timing and refresh behavior may reveal authorization activity.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile compatibility:** require same acceptance/rejection and **unchanged prior view after failure** for malformed compressed payload, expanded-size overflow, wrong authority/scope, same-epoch conflict, DIFF base mismatch, stale/clockless data, crash during staging/commit, cross-profile downgrade and decompression-failure overlap; keep the v1 vector set unchanged until a human authorizes a versioned format.
- **Required next evidence / disposition / weekly:** exact-head Rust/C offline compare or bounded prototype, per-profile input/expanded/scratch limits, decompression-failure and overflow fault corpus, temporal freshness/caching traces, STM32/ESP32-S3 peak RAM/latency/flash/power artifacts. **reproduce / benchmark; yes**.

A candidate is unfit if it fails *any* axis even when it compresses well.
