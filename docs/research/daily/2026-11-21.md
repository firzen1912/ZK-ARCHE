## 5. Material findings

### D20261121-F01 — Early remote-attestation evidence may not bind the final authenticated TLS application channel

- **Novelty:** newly published individual formal-analysis draft; refines optional R-006, with R-010 and R-015 cross-references.
- **Source:** Sardar et al., *Early Attestation Considered Very Harmful*, draft-intra-handshake-fail-63, individual Informational Internet-Draft, published **2026-11-20**.
- **Primary links:** https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-intra-handshake-fail-63 ; author-linked ProVerif artifacts: https://github.com/muhammad-usama-sardar/intra-handshake.fail .
- **Verified source-supported claim:** The authors distinguish correlation to the DH shared secret (G1), handshake traffic key (G2), and client application traffic key (G3). In their stated non-PSK TLS formal-model scope, seven assessed early-attestation binding constructions fail the G3 property; the early exporter reaches G1 but not G3. The draft includes ProVerif model/expected-output references and argues that later explicit nonce-bound evidence can achieve stronger authenticated-peer correlation. These are **authors' reported models/results**, not an independent reproduction here.
- **Engineering owner:** R-006; future optional attestation profile only; R-010 TLS binding, R-015 privacy and TD-003 formal claim boundary cross-references. Candidate roadmap zk228 and optional profile review, not an immediate normative change.
- **Repository fact:** spec/tls-exporter-channel-binding.md defines an optional **per-AUTH instance** exporter context and warns that TLS alone is not AUTH/TRUST authority; spec/privacy-considerations.md has no unconditional unlinkability claim; earlier November 12 research already compared LAKE RA-EDHOC message_3 early transcript and message_4 exporter semantics. Current ZK-ARCHE does **not** implement the modeled TLS early attestation protocol.
- **Bottleneck:** A valid device-attestation signature or binder tied only to a provisional handshake secret may be mistaken for proof of the final application peer and current authorization, enabling an unreviewed trust upgrade in a *future* optional implementation.
- **Distinct engineering idea:** Define an optional-attestation **phase/correlation ledger** for (attester key, authenticated peer, relying verifier, audience, nonce, final AUTH instance/transcript, exporter phase, TLS endpoint/hop, authorization generation). Qualify *both* a successful same-session positive witness and deliberately crossed/relayed parallel-handshake negative witnesses; compare early-transcript, regular exporter after Finished and explicit post-handshake nonce challenge without treating the TLS exporter as permission to ENROLL or release DATA.
- **Evidence maturity:** **formal** for the author's abstract claims; **concept** for ZK-ARCHE. No locally executed or independently verified proof, code or device data.
- **Limitations / uncertainty:** An individual Internet-Draft has no IETF endorsement. The paper's model assumptions and reported CVE/implementation claims have not been independently audited by this run. TLS TLS-Exporter phase and LAKE-RA-EDHOC message sequencing are different mechanisms: the report does **not** conclude LAKE is vulnerable, that all attestation is insecure, or that ZK-ARCHE has this attack. No PSK/resumption trace was tested here.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** Keep no-attestation native P2P and normal NO-LEARNING AUTH as mandatory reference; prohibit an early or foreign verifier result from implicitly authorizing a peer, group, enrollment or DATA action. Any attestation extension must state a **precise accepted key-confirmation frontier** and its session-binding invariant, plus loss/restart/clockless freshness rules.
- **Impact:** **Wire** none for mandatory suite; optional post-handshake challenge adds at least a nonce exchange/flight and signed evidence (exact bytes unknown). **CPU/RAM** evidence verification, additional challenge/transcript state and possible verifier workload. **Flash** key/trust anchors and freshness/rollback markers only if optional profile adopted. **Dependency** no mandatory EAT/TLS/PKI/cloud. **Trust** split attestation signer, verifier and local authorization. **Privacy** early evidence-type announcements, nonce reuse, verifier selection, measurements and timing can fingerprint classes or link sessions; R-015 must measure rather than assume.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile compatibility:** No current vector change. Proposed positive completed-channel attestation versus negative early phase, crossed client/server, wrong application/audience/nonce/attester key, foreign verifier, reused exporter/resumed channel, restart and authorization-revoked-before-effect vectors. Correlate with optional ProVerif relay canary and actual provider-pinned TLS output; no change to v2, draft AUTH-v3, iot-core or native suite 0x0001.
- **Next evidence / disposition:** Obtain/pin author's model source/results and a matched non-vacuous positive/negative ProVerif canary, then independently test the intended actual ZK-ARCHE optional binding design (if approved). **research-only** with R-006 last-reviewed evidence refined; weekly finding candidate **yes with explicit no-implementation/no-CVE caveat**.

### D20261121-F02 — SD-CWT interoperability exposes separate issuer, holder, disclosure and COSE-header trust boundaries

- **Novelty:** new comparative evidence reviewed from dated WG presentation; **refines** existing optional R-002 and cross-references R-015.
- **Sources/dates/type:** IETF SPICE WG draft-ietf-spice-sd-cwt-09, *Selective Disclosure CBOR Web Tokens*, published **2026-10-30**, WG Internet-Draft (not RFC); SPICE WG IETF 127 presentation **2026-11-20**; primary author PR #301 merged **2026-10-12** and PR #297 merged **2026-09-07**.
- **Primary links:** https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-spice-sd-cwt/09/ ; https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/127/materials/slides-127-spice-sd-cwt-update-00 ; https://github.com/ietf-wg-spice/draft-ietf-spice-sd-cwt/pull/301 ; https://github.com/ietf-wg-spice/draft-ietf-spice-sd-cwt/pull/297 .
- **Verified source-supported claim:** The November 20 presenters describe Python, JavaScript and Rust implementations and say the draft-09 tested interoperability profiles pass. Their **October main-branch snapshot, not a new November result**, records six canonical examples, 738 tests, 36 conformance bugs fixed and 40/40 interop checks (16 ordinary plus 24 AEAD). A normative holder-controlled sd_aead header was moved into the **unprotected** COSE header (PR #301); examples were corrected to hash byte-string-encoded disclosure material (PR #297). A contemporaneous October 18 WG mailing-list explanation states the holder-selected algorithm is covered by the holder Key Binding Token rather than by the issuer signature. These are reported and revision-verified upstream claims, not local Rust/C measurements.
- **Engineering owner:** R-002, R-015 cross-reference, optional credential ADR; TD-004 only if ever promoted. The native role-set proof and Common Contract remain authoritative for the baseline.
- **Repository fact:** R-002 currently compares single-use JWP and BBS issuance/presentation economics, holder binding and MCU costs; spec/privacy-considerations.md disallows inferring unlinkability from a credential's pseudonymity; spec/implementation-requirements.md requires canonical Rust/C parity, bounded decoding and appropriate negative vectors. No mandatory SD-CWT or COSE profile exists on current dev.
- **Bottleneck:** Protocol-correct selective disclosure requires more than accepting a signed issuer object: a verifier must distinguish its authenticated claim set from holder-controlled encrypted-disclosure algorithm metadata, canonical byte-level disclosure hashing, holder proof of possession/audience/nonce, and optional decryption success. Unprotected header metadata is not itself trusted identity, permission or secret evidence.
- **Distinct engineering idea:** Add SD-CWT as an **optional four-way comparative baseline**: native role-set proof, preissued single-use JWP, randomized BBS, and CBOR SD-CWT; use 1/10/100 offline presentations and exact canonical issuer/holder/disclosure fixtures. Tamper with unprotected sd_aead, duplicate/unknown/unsupported algorithm, mis-encoded bstr disclosures, incorrect digest, encrypted tag/nonce, missing holder binding, wrong audience, revoked issuer/subject, and replayed/rotating identifiers while observing whether independent verifier decisions agree.
- **Evidence maturity:** **software** for the presenters' independent upstream interoperability report (not independently rerun); **concept** for ZK-ARCHE adaptation. Draft remains non-final and optional.
- **Limitations / uncertainty:** The 738/40/40 figures refer to a defined **October** snapshot, not a ZK-ARCHE benchmark and not a combined November experiment. Passing examples do not establish robustness against every malformed input or privacy across transports. SD-CWT disclosure minimization is **not issuer-unobservability or general holder unlinkability**; presentation-level stable metadata may remain correlatable. Draft wording around header placement should be checked against the authoritative data model/revision when implementing, not inferred from a single prose occurrence.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** A measured, independently reproducible SD-CWT comparator may inform an optional lightweight selective-disclosure design, but should not displace current Schnorr/CDS-OR authentication or introduce issuer/key server/COSE/CBOR/BBS dependency to constrained P2P.
- **Impact:** **Wire** optional CBOR/COSE issuer signature, holder proof/key-binding material, disclosure arrays and optional AEAD (including tag/nonce) add overhead; exact per-presentation size unknown. **CPU** signatures, optional AEAD/CBOR/digests vs BBS pairings; **RAM** bounded CBOR nested parser, disclosed-claim and decryption buffers; **flash** issuer credential/inventory and CBOR/COSE implementation; **dependency** optional COSE/AEAD and key-binding support; **trust** explicit issuer/holder/verifier and local authorization separation; **privacy** issuer correlation, stable headers, disclosure-set and transport observer leakage must be measured.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile compatibility:** Pin a single draft commit and three-implementation fixture provenance; build deterministic valid and malformed CBOR/COSE/disclosure/AEAD/key-binding vectors; require equal Rust/C accept/reject with no unauthorized trust mutation, bounded parse RAM and flash, and no introduced native suite 0x0001 or current profile changes. Preserve optional-only off by default.
- **Next evidence / disposition:** Independently reproduce pinned upstream examples/cross-language accept/reject corpus, validate current draft header signatures and holder-binding semantics, measure complete bytes/MCU CPU/RAM/flash/energy and observer linkability before any human decision. R-002 stays **researching**, evidence contract refined. Weekly finding candidate **yes**.

## 6. Cross-source synthesis

Both primary-source families illustrate the same architectural limit from different directions: **valid cryptography on one artifact is not automatically end-to-end authority**. Early attestation may be sound at a DH/provisional-key level without binding the final application peer, while SD-CWT correctly authenticated issuer and holder artifacts require explicit treatment of presentation-time, potentially unprotected header choices and canonical disclosure bytes. ZK-ARCHE's separate local trust, audience, current authorization, exact AUTH instance, protected-effect and privacy boundaries remain appropriate. The optional comparisons do not create a mandatory issuer, verifier, attestation, CBOR/COSE, online or TLS dependency.
