## 2. Executive synthesis

1. **Cross-protocol privacy does not follow from per-transport pseudonym rotation.** The September 9 CrossLink research shows that unsynchronized temporary identifiers across LTE/Wi-Fi/BLE can be stitched into long-lived traces; its controlled-device and simulation evidence provides a concrete privacy failure mode, not a ZK-ARCHE measurement.
2. **Encrypted yes/no activity is itself an identifier candidate.** A 2026 peer-reviewed PoPETs study demonstrates reidentification from sparse binary interaction sequences, independently of content. ZK-ARCHE's AUTH/ENROLL/P2P outcome, silence, retry, timing, and DATA-release activity require explicit compositional analysis.
3. A future R-015 experiment should preserve security invariants: never weaken source validation, replay continuity, authorization failure behavior, or key binding solely for padding/traffic normalization. Compare bounded mitigations against wire/energy/latency and DoS budgets.
4. **R-015 remains `reproduce`.** No measured ZK-ARCHE linkability, countermeasure effectiveness, or general unlinkability assurance was established; all changes are research backlog evidence requirements.

## 5. Findings

### D20261010-F01 — Cross-transport observations defeat isolated pseudonym-rotation assumptions

- **Novelty:** new/refines; extends prior R-015 metadata and schema-fingerprinting work to *joint* multi-adapter observation.
- **Backlog owner:** R-015.
- **Source:** Aneet Kumar Dutta et al., *CrossLink: Breaking Location Privacy by Linking Device Identifiers Across Protocols*.
- **Primary link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09963
- **Source type / date:** original research preprint, 2026-09-09.
- **Verified source claim:** Separately temporary LTE, Wi-Fi, and BLE identifiers do not compose into location unlinkability under passive monitoring when rotation is unsynchronized. The authors report controlled commodity-device experiments and large-scale mobility simulation; within that simulation, full-trace reconstruction reached 83% versus 22% for the best single-protocol baseline. Partial, strategically placed observer coverage remained consequential.
- **Exact engineering owner:** R-015; `spec/privacy-considerations.md` sections 2, 3, 7–10; roadmap privacy work zk218/zk225/zk226/zk229/zk241 (human review only).
- **Repository fact:** ZK-ARCHE distinguishes protocol identity from transport addresses and already rejects general cross-transport unlinkability claims. Its retained privacy corpus has no published joint-observer adversarial trace experiment.
- **Problem addressed:** a user/device may rotate ZK pseudonyms while being trackable through independently scheduled radio/IP/TLS identities, connection activity, packet timing, and authentication/retry sessions.
- **Strongest distinct idea:** a **joint-observer, multi-adapter trace-reproduction contract** rather than isolated field checks. Generate or replay equivalent HELLO/AUTH/ENROLL/P2P traces across at least two adapters with independent/staggered pseudonym rotation, NAT/rebinding, session restart, and partial observer coverage; evaluate against identical single-adapter and randomized-linking baselines with explicit ground-truth identities.
- **Evidence maturity:** software/controlled experiments in the external work, with a simulation component; concept/reproduce for ZK-ARCHE.
- **Limitations:** The 83%/22% values are *not* ZK-ARCHE metrics and rely on the authors' simulated mobility/observer model. A device-to-person identity mapping or a global RF passive observer is not assumed by the mandatory ZK-ARCHE protocol. No conclusion that current ZK-ARCHE identifier rotation is vulnerable to this exact attack can be drawn without captures and target-specific modeling.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** explicitly scope any future unlinkability statement to observer coverage, transport composition, address/session behavior, rotation synchronization, and observation horizon. A cryptographically private PID is only one input to the composed system.
- **Wire / CPU / RAM / flash / dependency / trust / privacy impact:** the measurement-only phase adds no wire bytes, runtime crypto dependency, or trust authority. Candidate normalization/rotation/padding could cost bytes, flash/CPU/energy and add state/replay complexity; do not choose a mitigation without those measurements.
- **Rust/C / vector/profile compatibility:** existing deterministic proof vectors remain unchanged. Test harness should export *sanitized metadata-only* Rust/C traces with timestamp/size/direction/adapter/session/outcome, and check that equivalent semantic decisions produce comparable observables under specified profiles; do not record secrets or live identities. Per-adapter behavior must be compared independently of a canonical authenticated context.
- **Required next evidence:** reproducible labeled synthetic and, where authorized, laboratory packet traces; control/single/joint-observer baselines; link-success and false-positive distributions at specified population sizes/priors; concurrent/unsynchronized rotations, partial coverage and recovery/rebind conditions; retained local exact-head script/artifact hashes.
- **Recommended disposition:** reproduce.
- **Weekly finding candidate:** yes.

### D20261010-F02 — Encrypted completion/no-response patterns are quantifiable identity evidence

- **Novelty:** new/refines; goes beyond credential header, retry and error-byte distinguishability to a **time series of binary protocol activity**.
- **Backlog owner:** R-015; R-014 owns related lookup/oracle-cost behavior.
- **Source:** Christopher Ellis and Zhiqiang Lin, *Access Granted, Privacy Lost: Formalizing & Quantifying the Hidden Anonymity Risks of Exclusive-Use Systems*.
- **Primary link:** https://www.petsymposium.org/popets/2026/popets-2026-0069.php
- **Source type / date:** peer-reviewed Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies, vol. 2026, issue 3 (2026); venue lists artifact Available, Functional, Reproduced.
- **Verified source claim:** Sparse encrypted interaction/no-interaction patterns can distinguish users. In the authors' 16-user simulation derived from their network signal model, a passive observer obtained 54.7% Top-1 and 89.1% Top-3 reidentification and approximately 1.2 bits average entropy reduction out of the 4-bit prior. Study domains included Microsoft Teams activity, WhatsApp, and BLE-related attack context.
- **Exact engineering owner:** R-015 / `spec/privacy-considerations.md` sections 4, 5, 9, 10, 11; TD-004 normative error/privacy semantics; R-014 for lookup cost.
- **Repository fact:** AUTH NO-LEARNING prohibits implicit trust-store mutation, but it does not prohibit an observer inferring that a credential succeeded, a request completed, or a resumption/retry path was taken. The current repository explicitly says constant-time arithmetic is different from constant-observable protocol behavior.
- **Problem addressed:** even identical error strings and encrypted payloads may leak authorization/role/credential membership through response/silence, packet count, completion timing, retry frequency, or activity rhythms.
- **Strongest distinct idea:** measure **activity-only attribution** with content stripped: for each profile and endpoint/role class, derive timestamped binary success/response/no-response, retry, completion and DATA-eligibility sequences; use cross-session top-k classification accuracy, Bayes vulnerability, entropy loss, confusion/false-positive rates, and attacker horizon under balanced and skewed activity priors. Compare traffic-only, metadata-only, and combined attacker views.
- **Evidence maturity:** externally-reviewed publication with available/reproduced research artifact; no ZK-ARCHE reproduction (concept at project level).
- **Limitations:** the studied services and 16-user synthetic experiment do not establish real ZK-ARCHE device linkability or validate any proposed countermeasure. Source result figures depend on cohort, observer, activity model, and priors. Some silence patterns are essential to DoS/anti-amplification defense and cannot be indiscriminately made uniform.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** privacy-negative vector parity must cover *externally observable outcome sequences*, not only parsed bytes or uniform error code. Claim boundaries should distinguish protocol-message privacy, active membership probing, passive activity inference, and full fleet/deployment traffic analysis.
- **Wire / CPU / RAM / flash / dependency / trust / privacy impact:** initial classification on captured/synthetic traces adds no protocol overhead. Batching, jitter, padding, cover traffic, or delayed/error normalization would add radio bytes/latency/energy and may worsen loss, congestion, or DoS; the draft constrained 2048-byte datagram and retry/replay safety are hard evaluation constraints, not reasons to weaken authenticated failure behavior.
- **Rust/C / vector/profile compatibility:** no current wire/profile change. Recommended paired Rust/C metadata-only fixtures include valid/invalid/unknown/stale-authorization/NO-LEARNING/continuity-broken/fresh-vs-resumed/retry cases. Record both accept/reject **and** emitted/silent timing/length class; allow platform-specific timing only with calibrated limits and retained evidence.
- **Required next evidence:** reproducible outcome/time-series fixture generator; defined observer/cohort/priors/knowledge, statistical linkability thresholds and confidence intervals; branch-pair coverage for malformed/unknown/unauthorized/valid; privacy-vs-DoS tradeoff benchmark on physical constrained targets before any mitigation is proposed for adoption.
- **Recommended disposition:** reproduce.

## 6. Cross-source synthesis

RFC 6973 (July 2013), *Privacy Considerations for Internet Protocols*, already defines correlation and traffic analysis as multi-element inference that survives encryption. CrossLink supplies 2026 evidence for the **cross-adapter rotation/composition** failure mode; the 2026 PoPETs paper supplies a **time-series/binary activity** quantification technique. Both strengthen the existing `spec/privacy-considerations.md` warning but neither justifies general numerical risk claims without ZK-ARCHE-specific experiment design. A useful two-axis matrix is **single vs joint transport observer × packet metadata vs completion-activity traces**. Add observer reach/partial coverage and population priors as parameters. Physical RF fingerprinting and a global adversary remain outside baseline claims unless separately scoped.

Primary guidance comparator: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6973
