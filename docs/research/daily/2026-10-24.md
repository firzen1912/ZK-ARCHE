## 2. Executive synthesis

1. **A model-scope error can produce misleading assurance:** the two current ProVerif draft models use one attacker-supplied lifecycle classification per fresh association/release. They cannot demonstrate a protected operation before invalidation followed by another protected operation afterward on the **same retained association**.
2. The current negative queries prohibiting both `ProtectedUseAllowed(a)` and `AuthorizationGenerationStale(a)` are satisfied by mutually exclusive branches in this model, not by an implemented cross-operation invalidation transition. Simply adding a later stale event could instead make a timeless conjunction reject a legitimate *earlier* allow. A correctly ordered trace property is needed.
3. Tamarin documents linear/persistent state and explicit event timepoints; ProVerif research also supports temporal queries. The tool choice is subordinate to the R-004 canonical-model and faithful-translation decision, not a reason to impose a second unsynchronized normative model.
4. September 2026 primary research on **effect closure** adds a distinct runtime question: after authorization is invalidated, can already-admitted or queued work still reach an irreversible DATA-release effect? A classifier's return value is not a proof about downstream execution. The paper's cross-system results do **not** demonstrate a ZK-ARCHE vulnerability.
5. R-004 remains P0/`reproduce`. No formal proof, production claim, wire change or roadmap promotion resulted.

## 5. Findings

### D20261024-F01 — An ordered, non-vacuous repeated-use property is needed

- **Novelty / selected owner:** refines / R-004; technical-debt owner TD-003.
- **Primary sources, dates and types:** Tamarin Prover Manual, *Protocol Specification using Rules* and *Property Specification*, official project documentation (accessed 2026-10-24), https://tamarin-prover.com/manual/master/book/005_protocol-specification-rules.html and https://tamarin-prover.com/manual/master/book/007_property-specification.html ; Blanchet, Cheval & Cortier, *ProVerif with lemmas, induction, fast subsumption, and much more*, IEEE S&P (May 2022), official author publication page https://bblanche.gitlabpages.inria.fr/publications/BlanchetEtAlSP22.html .
- **Verified source facts:** Tamarin linear facts are consumed by transitions whereas persistent facts remain available; labeled action events admit trace properties with ordered timepoints (`i < j`) and existential executable traces. The peer-reviewed ProVerif work supports lemmas, induction and temporal queries. These are available verification techniques, not a proof of ZK-ARCHE.
- **Repository fact:** `rust/models/proverif/zk_arche_retained_authority_draft.pv` and `zk_arche_data_release_lifecycle_draft.pv` use mutually exclusive one-shot lifecycle branches and fresh association/release values; conjunction-based prohibition queries have no same-object before-and-after invalidation path. Existing C classifier tests do have mutate-and-reclassify evidence, but not stateful interleaving proof.
- **Problem:** Current success cannot substantiate post-invalidation retained-session *temporal* safety; a naive timeless prohibition might also incorrectly exclude legitimate prior protected effects in a richer model.
- **Distinct engineering idea:** Choose one canonical stateful model (Tamarin linear generation token **or** suitably stateful/temporal ProVerif, without unreviewed translation). Generate three required traces on the *same association and authority generation*: (a) positive allow before invalidation, (b) explicit invalidation/generation change, (c) denied attempt afterward. Express policy in ordered action events: `Invalidated(a,g)@i & ProtectedEffect(a,g)@j & i<j ==> false` **only for a profile that demands immediate post-invalidation effect exclusion**; otherwise represent its specified grace and effect-frontier semantics rather than asserting a stronger property.
- **Evidence maturity:** concept (tool semantics established externally; no new model constructed or executed).
- **Limits:** Temporal-model soundness depends on accurate mutation, persistence, rollback/restart and concurrency abstraction; neither syntactic trace formulas nor a successful vacuous proof establish compiled Rust/C behavior. The illustrative lemma is not a run-tested model.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** Require an `exists-trace` positive acceptance witness, an invalidation-ordering witness, and a deliberately weakened mutant that produces a counterexample; retain pinned model/tool hashes and exact-head command/output. Negative assurance alone is insufficient.
- **Wire/CPU/RAM/flash/dependency/trust/privacy:** research-only model adds no wire, target CPU/RAM/flash or mandatory dependency. Retained generation tokens or runtime atomic snapshots, if separately adopted, may require bounded RAM/CPU and possibly durable storage; no automatic expansion of trust authority or unlinkability claim.
- **Rust/C/vectors/profile impact:** add future scheduler-controlled same-association repeated-use corpus and model-event-to-spec-to-Rust/C mapping. Existing deterministic wire vectors and unselected AUTH-v3/iot-core profiles stay unchanged.
- **Required next evidence / disposition:** local exact-HEAD positive, rejection and faulty-mutant traces plus formal tool/version/assumptions manifest; `reproduce`; weekly finding candidate **yes**.

### D20261024-F02 — Revocation closure must be checked at the last preventable release effect

- **Novelty / selected owner:** new to queue / refines R-004; R-012 controls propagation/freshness of revocation facts, not this effect-boundary assurance question.
- **Primary source / date / type:** Igor Santos-Grueiro, *When Does Authorization End? Effect Closure at Provider Boundaries*, original research **preprint dated 2026-09-02**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02866 .
- **Verified source claim:** The paper distinguishes closing future authorization issuance from preventing previously authorized in-flight continuations reaching a now-forbidden effect. Its bounded contract/checker and Kafka experiments identify cases where completion or applied ACL changes do not by themselves establish effect closure. The results are conditional on modeled paths and interfaces and were not reproduced here.
- **Repository fact / bottleneck:** `c/tests/test_data_release_retained_association_temporal.c` checks `data_release_authorization_classify()` against mutable supplied inputs; the tested outcome is a decision, not an independently observed send/commit/storage boundary. `zk_arche_data_release_lifecycle_draft.pv` ends its abstraction at `ReleaseAllowed` or `ReleaseRejected`, not actual DATA release effect.
- **Strongest distinct idea:** Define a per-profile *effect frontier* for irreversible disclosure or durable DATA action, plus an authority-generation witness bound to the same request. Re-evaluate or atomically fence just before that frontier (and define handling for previously admitted in-flight work) rather than trusting an earlier allow decision. Explicitly distinguish *invalidation received locally* from *distributed revocation propagated* and an allowed bounded-offline stale window.
- **Evidence maturity:** concept for ZK-ARCHE; original research preprint with proof/checker claims and cross-system experiments, **not peer-reviewed ZK-ARCHE evidence**.
- **Limits/uncertainty:** Once plaintext DATA has been emitted, revocation cannot retrospectively undo disclosure; a policy may intentionally permit bounded in-flight completion. Correctness depends on precisely declared frontiers, transport buffering, persisted state, concurrency and cancellation capabilities. This is not a demonstrated ZK-ARCHE exploit.
- **ZK-ARCHE inference:** Build a local deterministic interleaving matrix: `read grant → invalidate → classify`, `classify → invalidate → enqueue`, `enqueue → invalidate → emit/commit`, duplicate/retry, restart/rollback, and valid successor association vs stale predecessor release; classify each against declared immediate or bounded-grace policy. Test whether stale-generation work is blocked before effect while independent authorized work continues.
- **Wire/CPU/RAM/flash/dependency/trust/privacy:** no research-stage wire change; prospective bounded generation snapshots, in-flight counters/fences, synchronization or durable checkpoints would cost CPU/RAM/latency, and nonvolatile generation would cost flash endurance. No new cryptographic dependency automatically justified. Blocking late disclosure could improve privacy; timing/error behavior could itself leak state and needs R-015 review.
- **Rust/C/vector/profile compatibility:** proposed unit/integration fixtures with the same authority event order for both lanes; actual payload emission or storage-commit observation required. Do not mark formal/code equivalence complete from classifier tests.
- **Required next evidence / disposition:** local exact-head scheduler-controlled Rust/C mutation-at-effect corpus, documented profile effect frontier and admissible in-flight policy; `reproduce`; weekly finding candidate **yes**.

## 6. Cross-source synthesis

Tamarin's temporal state machinery and ProVerif's temporal reasoning are candidates to test the *same-association* ordering missing from the current one-shot model. The effect-closure research adds the need to model not only authorization decisions but actual protected effects downstream. A credible result requires both non-vacuous successful and failing traces, exact model/tool provenance and Rust/C interleavings. This does not imply that either formal engine or the external paper has proven ZK-ARCHE's implementation secure or insecure.
